Terminology of Fine Watchmaking

The Horological Lexicon

A reference dictionary to ancestral artisanal decorations, mechanical complication architectures, and finishing standards.

Flying Tourbillon Complication

A cantilevered regulating cage supported exclusively from the baseplate, eliminating the traditional upper bridge to present an unobstructed view of the escapement in kinetic rotation.

Reference: Caliber ST-01 Flagship
Column Wheel Chronograph

A castellated mechanical cylinder machined with upright triangular teeth that coordinates the levers of start, stop, and reset actions with silky tactile precision.

Reference: Vanguard Caliber ST-204
Clous de Paris Dial Finition

A venerable engine-turned guilloché pattern composed of intersecting miniature pyramidal studs cut individually onto solid precious metal dial blanks via hand-operated rose lathes.

Reference: Heritage Automatic ST-301
Mirror Anglage Artisanal Finish

The meticulous hand-chamfering of internal and external bridge edges to a pristine 45-degree angle, polished to a distortion-free optical mirror shine using gentian wood pegs and diamond paste.

Reference: Artisan Skeleton Caliber ST-701
Silicon Balance Spring Chronometry

A micro-etched monocrystalline hairspring totally immune to magnetic fields up to 15,000 Gauss and thermal variance, ensuring isochronous oscillation under extreme environmental changes.

Reference: Nautilus Diver Pro ST-402
Perpetual Memory Grand Complication

A 210-part mechanical computer employing a 48-month notched wheel that accurately accounts for 30- and 31-day months as well as leap year February 29ths without manual adjustment until the secular year 2100.

Reference: Imperium Perpetual ST-880

Frequently Explored Collector Inquiries

Insights directly from our Geneva atelier service desk regarding caliber preservation, magnetism, and wearing etiquette.

In stationary pocket watches, gravity pulled steadily on the hairspring in a single downward vector, causing rate fluctuations. In a modern wristwatch, while arm motion naturally varies position, the single-axis tourbillon constantly averages out positional delta rates by revolving the entire balance assembly 360 degrees once every 60 seconds, achieving supreme isochronism.

Silicon (Silicium) is completely non-magnetic, unaffected by smartphones, induction hobs, or magnetic bag clasps that cause steel balance coils to stick together and gain hours per day. Furthermore, silicon is lightweight, thermal-neutral, and can be etched with a raised terminal curve for optimal concentric breathing.

Platinum used in our cases is 95% pure (950 purity) compared to 75% for 18K gold. Platinum has significantly higher density (meaning a distinct, reassuring heft on the wrist) and does not wear away when scratched; rather, the metal displaces and develops a rich, satin patina cherished by connoisseurs.

For manual-wind pieces like CHRONOS TYPE-0, wind smoothly forward until you feel a firm resistance stop—never force past this point. For automatic references, 25-30 forward crown turns will initiate full amplitude from a dead stop, after which normal wrist motion maintains maximum barrel tension.
